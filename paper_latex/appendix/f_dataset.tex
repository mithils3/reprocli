\section{Dataset Schema}
\label{app:dataset-schema}

This section records the schema of the frozen lockfile, the artifact the
selection pipeline of Section~\ref{subsec:construction} writes and every run
reads.

% src: outputs/v6/app_rebuild/eval_100.jsonl, outputs/v6/app_rebuild/dev_split.jsonl,
%      outputs/v6/app_rebuild/splits_summary.json, notes/DATASET.md 8,
%      paper_latex/tables/gen_eval100.py (summary output)
We submit the audited benchmark as anonymized supplementary material, packaged
so that the \texttt{datasets} library loads it by name. The dataset carries two
splits and there is no \texttt{train} split. The \texttt{test} split holds the 100-paper
frozen benchmark and carries \texttt{split="eval"} in every row, 34 papers at
Run tier, 33 at Retrain, and 33 at Reimplement. The \texttt{validation} split
holds the 14-paper development set and carries \texttt{split="dev"}, 5 at Run,
4 at Retrain, and 5 at Reimplement. No arXiv identifier appears in both splits.
Each row is one paper with 25 top-level fields, keyed by its arXiv identifier
in \texttt{custom\_id}. The row records the paper's central claim, the audited
artifact-availability signals, the reproduction task handed to the agent, the
pinned match target, and the compute estimate that placed the paper in its
band.

% src: outputs/v6/app_rebuild/eval_100.jsonl, notes/DATASET.md 8
Table~\ref{tab:schema-toplevel} lists the top-level fields. Four of them are
nested objects, expanded in
Tables~\ref{tab:schema-links}--\ref{tab:schema-h100}. Together they fix the
claim, the artifact evidence, the success bar, and the compute budget before
any agent starts, so a score from one agent is measured against the same ruler
as a score from another.

Four schema invariants hold across the released rows, and a reader can check
each one from the file. The tier is a function of the score. Score 0 maps to
Run, score 1 maps to Retrain, and scores 2 and 3 map to Reimplement.
\texttt{selection\_band} and \texttt{h100\_band} agree on every row, so the
band a paper was sampled in is the band its audited estimate falls in.
\texttt{audited\_h100\_hours} departs from \texttt{h100\_estimate.hours} on
exactly the 6 rows, 3 in each split, whose \texttt{h100\_hours\_adjudicated}
flag is set. Of the
400 signal entries in the evaluation split, 388 record a tool call and 12 are
\texttt{not\_applicable}. Every signal set true is backed by a successful tool
call, with one exception. On 2503.02809 the pinned configuration runs on
synthetic data, so \texttt{dataset\_available} is true and
\texttt{not\_applicable} at once. Across both splits 13 signals are
\texttt{not\_applicable}, 12 of them with a false value, and 10 of those 12 are
\texttt{weights\_available} on papers whose pinned configuration needs no
released checkpoint.

\paragraph{Match bars and tolerance.} For 62 of the 100 evaluation papers the
match bar is a point estimate. For 21 the run must beat a stated baseline, for
14 it must clear a reported threshold, for 1 it must recover the size of a
reported delta, and for 1 it must land inside a reported range. One row,
2505.10475, entered the split as a late replacement and carries no
\texttt{match\_bar\_kind} field. The \texttt{range} kind is also absent from
the auditor's output enumeration, which admits point estimate, threshold,
direction, magnitude, and none, so the auditor records that row's bar under the
nearest admitted kind. A point estimate matches when the run lands within the authors'
stated tolerance, or within 5\% relative of the reported value when the paper
states none. A direction bar is a strict inequality and admits no tolerance
band, so a one-point win in the stated direction counts.

\paragraph{Loading the dataset.} Listing~\ref{lst:load-dataset} loads both
splits and prints the pinned match target of the running example. The
identifier is masked for anonymous review. The rows carry metadata and links,
so a load returns the fields of Table~\ref{tab:schema-toplevel} and no paper
text. A replicator fetches the paper and the artifacts from the URLs in
\texttt{verified\_links}.

\begin{codebox}[lst:load-dataset]{Python}{Loading the two splits and reading one pinned match target. The dataset identifier is masked for anonymous review.}
from datasets import load_dataset

eval100 = load_dataset("<anonymized>/reclaim", split="test")
dev14 = load_dataset("<anonymized>/reclaim", split="validation")

row = eval100.filter(lambda r: r["custom_id"] == "2506.20990")[0]
print(row["tier"], row["h100_band"], row["audited_h100_hours"])
print(row["match_target"])
\end{codebox}

\paragraph{Per-paper anchors.} Table~\ref{tab:eval100} lists the 100
evaluation papers with the anchor each one is graded against, and
Table~\ref{tab:dev14} does the same for the 14 development papers. The
evaluation split totals 1{,}153.8 audited H100-hours, 142.71 at Run
tier, 380.91 at Retrain, and 630.18 at Reimplement, and the development split
totals 26.89. Within a tier the compute bands run 27, 7, and 0 papers at Run,
16, 13, and 4 at Retrain, and 16, 9, and 8 at Reimplement, reading the
\texttt{0-8}, \texttt{8-32}, and \texttt{32-96} bands in order. Eight of the 12
evaluation papers in the \texttt{32-96} band sit at Reimplement tier, so the
tier that asks for the most work also asks for the most compute. A reader can
recompute any per-tier or per-band figure we report from these two tables and
the run records in the supplementary material.
