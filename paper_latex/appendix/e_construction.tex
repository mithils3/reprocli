\section{Dataset Construction Details}
\label{app:construction}

% src: iclr2027_conference.tex:319-396 (subsec:construction, fig:funnel); notes/DATASET.md:114-441
This appendix records the construction funnel summarized in
Figure~\ref{fig:funnel}, from 1,000 classified papers through a 200-paper audit
pool to the 100-paper evaluation split.

\subsection{Source corpus}
\label{app:corpus}

% src: src/reprocli_data/pipeline/index.py:1-4,18-24,49-75; src/reprocli_data/pipeline/sources.py:32-60,115; src/reprocli_data/pipeline/supplements.py:1-5,85-175,184-233; src/reprocli_data/pipeline/bundle.py:30-56,150-210; src/reprocli_data/build_dataset.py:119-160; notes/DATASET.md:114-146
The corpus starts from the NeurIPS 2025 index of the \emph{ai-conferences}
collection~\citep{neurips2025corpus}, in which each entry already carries a
matched arXiv ID. We keep a row only when that ID is well formed, and at no
stage do we pair a paper with an arXiv entry by title. Two fetch stages then
retrieve each paper's arXiv source package and its OpenReview supplement, the
latter matched by forum identifier, and the bundle stage writes one row per
paper containing the decoded \texttt{.tex} text and the supplement files. The
resulting snapshot holds 3,414 bundled papers, of which 3,410 have at least one
\texttt{.tex} file and 1,547 have a supplement.

\subsection{Stage I: artifact-verified classification}
\label{app:classifier}

% src: src/reprocli_vllm/config/config.py:7-13,16,28-32; src/reprocli_serve/profiles.py:65-74; src/reprocli_vllm/tools/web_tools.py:22-32 at commit 96d7f7f^; src/reprocli_vllm/runtime/loop_guards.py:1-66; src/reprocli_vllm/runtime/tool_loop.py:126-129,184-215; src/reprocli_vllm/runtime/run_health.py:17-27,29-46,57-70; src/reprocli_vllm/schema/output.py:8-20,92-154; prompts/prompt.txt:33-63,324-333; notes/DATASET.md:156-261
We sampled 1,000 papers uniformly at random from the corpus rows that have
LaTeX source. MiniMax-M2.7~\citep{minimax2026m2}, which we served on vLLM
at a 196,608-token context with requests capped at 128,000 input tokens,
classified them on 2026-06-11. For each paper the classifier runs an agent
loop over ten tools: five over GitHub, three over Hugging Face, a URL fetch,
and a supplement reader. Every artifact signal records how a tool established
it. The loop is bounded by three guards, a 10-round cap, a repeated-call
cutoff, and a context budget, and the verification status records each one. A
row enters the pool only
if all of its applicable signals were established by tool calls and the loop
exited naturally. The final, tool-free turn returns a strict JSON record that
contains neither a score nor a tier, and the saved tool conversation lets us
re-open any individual call afterwards. Figure~\ref{lst:classifier-prompt}
gives the classifier prompt in full, with \texttt{\{PAPER\_TEXT\}} standing for
the rendered paper, its supplement manifest with text excerpts, and its
LaTeX source.

\subsection{Deterministic extraction}
\label{app:extraction}

% src: src/reprocli_vllm/schema/output.py:157-225; src/reprocli_vllm/audit/h100.py:1-95; prompts/prompt.txt:390-420,456-483; notes/DATASET.md:228-272
Score and tier are computed in code from the four signal booleans. Missing
code adds 2, missing weights adds 1, and missing data adds 3 unless the dataset
is a standard public benchmark. A score of 0 maps to \texttt{Easy}, 1 to
\texttt{Medium}, and 2 to \texttt{Hard}, which are the released dataset's tier
values for Run, Retrain, and Reimplement. A score of 3 stays \texttt{Hard} when
the data are available or standard and is otherwise \texttt{Artifact-Blocked},
as is any higher score. The extraction code also re-derives the compute
estimate as GPU count times wall-clock hours times the multiplier of
Table~\ref{tab:h100-multipliers}, and flags a mismatch when the recomputed and
stated figures differ by more than 20\% of the larger. It assigns a band from
the ladder 0--8, 8--32, 32--96, and above 96 H100-hours, and raises a
human-review flag whenever the basis is unspecified or the recompute
disagrees.

\subsection{Stage II: compute capping and stratified selection}
\label{app:selection}

% src: src/reprocli_vllm/audit/select_pool.py:39-134; notes/DATASET.md:289-336,360-372
The selection pass admits a row only if its verification status is verified,
its tier is one of the three evaluation tiers, and its audited compute is
known and under the cap. Audited compute is the recomputed product when the
arithmetic audit flagged a mismatch and the multiplier is at or below 1.1, and
the stated hours otherwise. Of the 620 verified rows in those tiers, 38 lay
above the ceiling used by the selection pass, leaving 582 to enter selection.
That ceiling was higher than the benchmark's eventual cap, which is why 17 pool
rows lie above 96 H100-hours. We lowered the cap to 96 on 2026-07-09, and the
three evaluation-split papers above it were replaced at that point
(Table~\ref{tab:swaps}). The selection rule itself divides the pool target
equally across tiers by largest remainder and apportions each tier's share
across compute bands by fixed weights. Within a band it takes the cheapest rows
first, breaking ties by arXiv ID, and it fills the most expensive band first so
that any deficit cascades down into the cheaper bands of the same tier.
Table~\ref{tab:audit-pool} reports the resulting pool, which is twice the
size of the final benchmark, a margin that absorbs the attrition of the human
audit and leaves a replacement reserve in every tier.

\subsection{Stage III: human audit}
\label{app:human-audit}

% src: tools/verify_app/README.md:1-16,36-82; tools/rebuild_splits_from_app.py:110-165; src/reprocli_openai/recheck.py:1-12,30-45,89-95,118-125,247-266; notes/DATASET.md:337-359
For every paper in the pool we opened the evidence behind each availability
call and recorded a verdict on each of the four signals. The reviewer never
types a score. Instead, a review form recomputes it from the verdicts using
the formula of Appendix~\ref{app:extraction}, and compute bands are not
re-judged at this stage. Verdicts override the model one signal at a time. A
disagreement flips that signal's boolean and anything else leaves it as it
was, after which the deterministic scorer recomputes score and tier. Because a
Reimplement-tier assignment rests on no code having been located, we
re-checked those rows with GPT-5.5 under web search, and on 2026-07-13 we
submitted each claimed wall in that tier to one adversarial prober and two
independent refuters.

\subsection{The evaluation and development splits}
\label{app:splits}

% src: tools/rebuild_splits_from_app.py:1-22,44-93,166-205; notes/DATASET.md:360-372
The split builder applies the human verdicts to the model values the reviewers
saw, recomputes score and tier, drops any paper whose minimal experiment calls
a paid or closed model API, and runs the Stage II selection rule over what
remains. The development split is then drawn from the cheapest eligible papers
left in each tier, so the two splits are disjoint by construction.
Appendix~\ref{app:dataset-schema} gives the split sizes.

\subsection{Compute re-estimation}
\label{app:recompute}

% src: tasks/h100-recompute-eval100.md:1-12,25-60,199-236
After the split was first released we re-derived every evaluation row's
H100-hour figure, in a pass completed on 2026-07-01. The figure is the compute
of the minimal reproducible experiment, evaluation-only when the weights are
released and otherwise the smallest training run that shows the effect. We
recomputed each row as GPU count times wall-clock hours times the multiplier
of Table~\ref{tab:h100-multipliers}, dividing any reported total GPU-hour
figure by the GPU count first. Ninety of the 100 rows changed. The audited
total fell from 4,426 to 1,922 H100-hours, and the median row from 35 to 2.5
hours. Four rows have released weights that are only the base model, and we
kept them as small training runs.

be unreproducible in the sandbox by construction. A model failure never
removes a paper. Each replacement is the next-cheapest audited pool row of the
