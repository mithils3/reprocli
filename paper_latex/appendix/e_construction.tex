\section{Dataset Construction Details}
\label{app:construction}

\subsection{Source corpus}
\label{app:corpus}

The corpus starts from the NeurIPS 2025 index of the \emph{ai-conferences}
collection~\citep{neurips2025corpus}, whose entries have matched arXiv IDs; we
keep a row only when that ID is well formed, and we never pair by title. Two
fetch stages pull each paper's arXiv source package and its OpenReview supplement,
matched by forum identifier, into one bundle row per paper with the decoded
\texttt{.tex} text and the supplement files, 3,414 bundled papers, 3,410 with
a \texttt{.tex} file and 1,547 with a supplement.

\subsection{Stage I: artifact-verified classification}
\label{app:classifier}

We sampled 1,000 papers uniformly from the corpus rows with LaTeX source, and
MiniMax-M2.7~\citep{minimax2026m2}, served on vLLM at a 196,608-token context
with requests capped at 128,000 input tokens, classified them on 2026-06-11.
Each classification runs an agent loop over ten tools, five over GitHub, three
over Hugging Face, a URL fetch, and a supplement reader, and every artifact
signal records how a tool established it. Three guards bound the loop: a
10-round cap, a repeated-call cutoff, and a context budget. Each is recorded in
the verification status, and a row enters the pool only if every applicable
signal was tool-established and the loop exited naturally. The final tool-free turn
returns strict JSON with neither score nor tier, and the saved tool
conversation lets us re-open any call. The prompt is
Figure~\ref{lst:classifier-prompt}, where \texttt{\{PAPER\_TEXT\}} stands for
the rendered paper, its supplement manifest with text excerpts, and its LaTeX
source.

\subsection{Deterministic extraction}
\label{app:extraction}

Code computes score and tier from the four signal booleans. Missing code adds
2, missing weights 1, and missing data 3 unless the dataset is a standard
public benchmark; scores of 0, 1, and 2 map to \texttt{Easy} (Run),
\texttt{Medium} (Retrain), and \texttt{Hard} (Reimplement), and 3 stays
\texttt{Hard} when the data are available or standard and is otherwise
\texttt{Artifact-Blocked}, as is any higher score. The same code re-derives
compute as GPU count times wall-clock hours times the multiplier of
Table~\ref{tab:h100-multipliers}, flags a mismatch when the recomputed and
stated figures differ by more than 20\% of the larger, assigns a band from the
ladder 0--8, 8--32, 32--96, and above 96 H100-hours, and raises a human-review
flag when the basis is unspecified or the recompute disagrees.

\subsection{Stage II: compute capping and stratified selection}
\label{app:selection}

Selection admits a row whose verification status is verified, whose tier is
one of the three evaluation tiers, and whose audited compute is known and
under the cap, taking the recomputed product when the arithmetic audit flagged
a mismatch and the multiplier is at or below 1.1 and the stated hours
otherwise. Of 620 verified rows in those tiers, 38 lay above the selection
ceiling, leaving 582; because that ceiling was higher than the eventual cap,
17 pool rows lie above 96 H100-hours, and we lowered the cap to 96 on
2026-07-09, replacing the three evaluation-split papers above it
(Table~\ref{tab:swaps}). The rule divides the pool target equally across tiers
by largest remainder, apportions each tier's share across bands by fixed
weights, takes the cheapest rows first with ties by arXiv ID, and fills the
most expensive band first so deficits cascade down into cheaper bands of the
same tier. At 200 rows the pool (Table~\ref{tab:audit-pool}) is twice the
benchmark, absorbing the attrition of the human audit and leaving a
replacement reserve in every tier.

\subsection{Stage III: human audit}
\label{app:human-audit}

For every pool paper we opened the evidence behind each availability call and
recorded a verdict on each of the four signals. The reviewer never types a
score; the review form recomputes it from the verdicts by the formula of
Appendix~\ref{app:extraction}, and compute bands are not re-judged. Verdicts
override the model one signal at a time: a disagreement flips that signal's
boolean, leaves the other signals as they were, and the deterministic scorer
recomputes score and tier. Because a Reimplement-tier assignment rests on no
code having been located, we re-checked those rows with
GPT-5.5~\citep{openai2026gpt55} under web search, and on 2026-07-13 we
submitted every claimed wall in that tier to one adversarial prober and two
independent refuters.

\subsection{The evaluation and development splits}
\label{app:splits}

The split builder applies the human verdicts to the model values the reviewers
saw, recomputes score and tier, drops any paper whose minimal experiment calls
a paid or closed model API, and runs the Stage II selection rule over what
remains. The development split is drawn from the cheapest eligible papers left
in each tier, so the two splits are disjoint by construction, and
Appendix~\ref{app:dataset-schema} gives their sizes.

\subsection{Compute re-estimation}
\label{app:recompute}

After the split was first released, a pass completed on 2026-07-01 re-derived every evaluation row's H100-hour
figure, the compute of the minimal reproducible experiment, evaluation-only
when the weights are released and otherwise the smallest training run that
shows the effect. Each row is GPU count times wall-clock hours times the
multiplier of Table~\ref{tab:h100-multipliers}, with any reported total
GPU-hour figure divided by the GPU count first. Ninety of the 100 rows
changed, the audited total fell from 4,426 to 1,922 H100-hours and the median
row from 35 to 2.5 hours, and we kept the four rows whose released weights are
only the base model as small training runs.

\subsection{Changes to the evaluation split since release}
\label{app:substitutions}

be unreproducible in the sandbox by construction, and a model failure never
removes one. Each replacement is the next-cheapest audited pool row of the
same tier (Table~\ref{tab:swaps}).
