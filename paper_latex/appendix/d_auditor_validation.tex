\section{Auditor Validation}
\label{app:auditor-validation}

This appendix records what is known about grader noise in the audit scores of
Section~\ref{sec:auditing}, measured by re-grading sweeps that had already been
graded once.

\subsection{What pinning the auditor fixes}

% src: src/reprocli_claude/__main__.py:1-19,74-81,265-276, src/reprocli_vllm/config/config.py:42-43, rubric_audit.md:1-15, src/reprocli_vllm/audit/audit.py:35-63
Four inputs are held constant across every grade of record. The grader model id
is one command-line argument, and it is written onto every verdict row it
produces. The rubric is loaded from a single file that carries its own freeze
date of 16 July 2026, and the audit prompt is a template rendered per run with
that rubric text, the pinned claim, and the run's file manifest. The finalizer
that turns a graded response into a row is shared with the in-sweep auditor, so
the integrity adjustment of Appendix~\ref{app:rubric} is applied by one piece of
code for every pass. Appendix~\ref{app:models} gives the grading client, its
tools, and its decoding settings.

% src: src/reprocli_claude/__main__.py:193-201,282-295, src/reprocli_vllm/runtime/audit_sink.py:85-104,124-129, notes/Analysis/2026-07-23 Pinned Sonnet-5 Replay (sweep 2687371).md:18-28
A grading pass also carries an identity of its own, the grader model name joined
to a UTC timestamp, and every row and transcript event the pass writes is keyed
by it. That tag is what makes a second pass observable at all. Without it a
re-grade resolves to the first pass's row, inherits the older verdict, and loses
its own transcript to key collisions on the events already there. The verdict
store therefore holds one row per run and grader, while the run row holds a
single headline verdict that a later pass overwrites only when the pass is asked
to upload.

\subsection{Protocols}

% src: notes/Analysis/2026-07-23 Pinned Sonnet-5 Replay (sweep 2687371).md:9-11, notes/Analysis/2026-08-03 Medium Sweep 2690187 (MiniMax, pinned Sonnet).md:9-10,18-27, notes/Analysis/2026-07-20 Easy Sweep 2652648 (MiniMax repair).md:18-20, notes/Analysis/easy-sweep-2687371-qwen3-runs.json, notes/Analysis/medium-sweep-2690187-minimax-runs.json, rubric_audit.md:1-5
Two validations re-grade runs that had already been graded. No run is
re-executed in either of them, so the evidence bundles, the pinned claims, the
frozen rubric text, and the score scale are identical in both arms of each
comparison. Both change grader identity, pairing the sweep's original grades
against a later pass by the pinned auditor. Both sweeps ran after the 13 July
2026 dataset freeze, the pinned arm of each is graded by Claude Sonnet 5, and
one amendment to the audit prompt separates the two arms of the first
comparison.

% src: iclr2027_conference.tex:385-390, notes/Analysis/2026-08-03 Medium Sweep 2690187 (MiniMax, pinned Sonnet).md:12-16,25-27
The other arm of each comparison is a grade the agent model produced
over its own family's runs, and it is not a result of this paper. Every score
reported in Section~\ref{subsec:results} is a pinned-auditor grade, because we
fix one grader for every run of every agent and no agent grades its own family
(Section~\ref{sec:auditing}). Those earlier grades are kept here because they
are the only second reading of the same transcripts that exists, which is what
makes them usable as a measurement of grader spread. Table~\ref{tab:auditor-validation}
summarizes the two comparisons.

\subsection{Changing the grader on an easy-tier sweep}

% src: notes/Analysis/2026-07-23 Pinned Sonnet-5 Replay (sweep 2687371).md:9-11,107-118
An easy-tier sweep of Qwen3.6-27B was graded by that model during the sweep and
re-graded by the pinned auditor on 23 July 2026, 34 runs of 34. Both graders
scored 32 of those runs, and the paired comparison uses that set. The mean score
is 4.66 under the sweep's own grader and 3.97 under the pinned auditor. The
sweep's grader scored higher on 10 runs, equal on 20, and lower on 2, and the
mean difference of 0.69 carries a Wilcoxon $p = 0.0108$. Across all 34 pinned
audits the mean is 3.91.

% src: notes/Analysis/2026-07-23 Pinned Sonnet-5 Replay (sweep 2687371).md:109-114,132-142,147-158
The reproduced count does not move. Five runs are graded reproduced under each
grader, and the membership of that set moves by two, with one run losing the
verdict and one gaining it. The disqualified count moves from 2 to 6, and the
count of runs carrying a high-severity flag moves with it from 2 to 6. All four
added disqualifications are flagged \texttt{other\_provenance\_break} or
\texttt{wrong\_split\_scale\_dataset}, and each cites a specific break, such as
a report claiming a 2{,}427-sample evaluation whose only backed value comes from
a 5-sample probe.

% src: notes/Analysis/2026-07-23 Pinned Sonnet-5 Replay (sweep 2687371).md:120-131, prompts/prompt_audit.txt:88-101
One thing besides grader identity separates the two arms of this comparison. The
audit prompt's severity rules were amended on 22 July 2026 to rule that a
released results file which the agent consumes and discloses caps the score band
without raising a flag. 32 of the 33 grades in the first arm predate that
amendment and all 34 pinned audits postdate it, so grader identity and prompt
version moved together. Reading the flag payloads separates them. The amended
rule fired on exactly one run of 34 and it fired at low severity, which does not
reach the score-0 cap, so the rest of the difference is attributable to the
grader.

\subsection{Changing the grader on a medium-tier sweep}

% src: notes/Analysis/2026-08-03 Medium Sweep 2690187 (MiniMax, pinned Sonnet).md:9-10,25-27,266-277, notes/Analysis/medium-sweep-2690187-minimax-runs.json
A medium-tier sweep of MiniMax-M2.7 was graded the same way and re-graded by the
pinned auditor on 3 August 2026, 32 runs of 32. Six runs are graded reproduced
by the sweep's own grader and 5 by the pinned auditor, and the pinned set is a
subset of the earlier one with no run promoted into it. Both graders disqualify
exactly 10 runs and they agree on 4 of those. Six papers are disqualified only
by the sweep's grader and six only by the pinned auditor.

% src: notes/Analysis/2026-08-03 Medium Sweep 2690187 (MiniMax, pinned Sonnet).md:277-284
The mean score moves from 3.91 to 3.41 over the same 32 transcripts. The verdict
changes on 16 of the 32 runs and the score changes on 20. Score correlation
across all 32 is $r = 0.657$. Across the 16 runs where neither grader applied
the score-0 cap it is $r = 0.863$, with subset means of 6.12 and 5.69.

\subsection{Where the disagreement sits}

% src: notes/Analysis/2026-08-03 Medium Sweep 2690187 (MiniMax, pinned Sonnet).md:286-291, notes/Analysis/2026-07-23 Pinned Sonnet-5 Replay (sweep 2687371).md:147-158, notes/Analysis/2026-08-13 Hard Sweep 2918306 (DSv4, pinned Sonnet).md:456
The two measurements put the disagreement in one place. The continuous score
is reliable where the cap does not fire, at $r = 0.863$ over the runs neither
grader capped. The top of the scale is stable, with 5 of the 6 earlier
reproductions surviving the swap as a strict subset and no run promoted into it.
The score-0 cap is the unstable part, shared on 4 of 10 disqualifications when
the grader changes. No second pass of the pinned auditor over one sweep is on
record, so the within-grader stability of the cap is unmeasured.

% src: notes/Analysis/2026-08-03 Medium Sweep 2690187 (MiniMax, pinned Sonnet).md:293-300,332-335
Two reporting rules follow, and we apply both. We report a disqualification
count as a rubric behaviour with this instability attached, since 4 of 10
disqualifications are shared between the two graders of the medium-tier sweep.
We compare a mean audit score only within one grader, since the grader swap
alone moves this sweep's mean by 0.5 points over identical transcripts.

\subsection{Human validation}

% src: iclr2027_conference.tex:413-415, notes/Writing/Section Pointers.md:20, notes/TODO List.md:86-87
The expert-grader study is pending and its result is the agreement figure quoted
in Section~\ref{sec:auditing}. The design on file is to have expert human
graders score a held-out sample of runs alongside cases carrying injected
provenance breaks, and to report agreement as a kappa and an F1 against the
human labels together with recall on the injected cases. Until that study runs,
the code-enforced cap is evidence that a flagged run cannot pass and is no
evidence about a flag the auditor missed or a score it set too harshly.

\subsection{Which statistics survive}

% src: notes/Analysis/2026-07-23 Pinned Sonnet-5 Replay (sweep 2687371).md:147-158, notes/Analysis/2026-08-03 Medium Sweep 2690187 (MiniMax, pinned Sonnet).md:293-300
A reader of Section~\ref{subsec:results} should treat the reproduced count as
the statistic that survives a change of grader. It is identical under both
graders of the easy-tier sweep, and it loses one run as a strict subset under
the medium-tier swap. The disqualification count is the statistic that does not
survive, moving from 2 to 6 on one sweep and holding its total while exchanging
six of its ten members on the other. The mean score sits between them and carries a grader effect of
0.69 and 0.5 points on the two sweeps, so we read it within one grader
only.
