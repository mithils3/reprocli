\section{Auditor Validation}
\label{app:auditor-validation}

% src: notes/Analysis/2026-07-23 Pinned Sonnet-5 Replay (sweep 2687371).md, notes/Analysis/2026-08-03 Medium Sweep 2690187 (MiniMax, pinned Sonnet).md
This appendix reports what we know about grader noise in the audit scores of
Section~\ref{sec:auditing}, measured by re-grading sweeps that had already been
graded once. Throughout, a \emph{grader} is any model that scored a run, and
the \emph{auditor} is the pinned grader whose scores the paper reports.

\subsection{Why the auditor is pinned}

% src: src/reprocli_claude/__main__.py:1-19,74-81,265-276, src/reprocli_vllm/config/config.py:42-43, rubric_audit.md:1-15, src/reprocli_vllm/audit/audit.py:35-63
We hold four inputs constant across every grade of record. They are the grader
model ID, which is written onto every verdict row, the rubric file with its
2026-07-16 freeze date, the audit prompt rendered per run from that rubric
text and the pinned claim, and the shared verdict code that applies the
integrity adjustment of Appendix~\ref{app:rubric}. The grading client and its
decoding settings are given in Appendix~\ref{app:models}.

% src: src/reprocli_claude/__main__.py:193-201,282-295, src/reprocli_vllm/runtime/audit_sink.py:85-104,124-129, notes/Analysis/2026-07-23 Pinned Sonnet-5 Replay (sweep 2687371).md:18-28
Each grading pass is also given an identity of its own, formed from the grader
model name and a UTC timestamp, and every row and transcript event the pass
writes is keyed by it. The identity is what makes a second pass observable.
Without a distinct identity, a re-grade would resolve to the first pass's row
and inherit the older verdict.

\subsection{Validation protocol}

% src: notes/Analysis/2026-07-23 Pinned Sonnet-5 Replay (sweep 2687371).md:9-11, notes/Analysis/2026-08-03 Medium Sweep 2690187 (MiniMax, pinned Sonnet).md:9-10,18-27, notes/Analysis/2026-07-20 Easy Sweep 2652648 (MiniMax repair).md:18-20, notes/Analysis/easy-sweep-2687371-qwen3-runs.json, notes/Analysis/medium-sweep-2690187-minimax-runs.json, rubric_audit.md:1-5
Both validations re-grade existing runs without re-executing them, so the run
bundles, the pinned claims, the frozen rubric text, and the score scale are
identical in the two arms and only the grader changes. Both sweeps ran after
the 2026-07-13 dataset freeze, and in each the pinned arm is graded by Claude
Sonnet 5.

The other arm in each comparison is a grade that the agent model itself
produced over its own family's runs during the sweep. Those grades are not
results of this paper: every score in Section~\ref{subsec:results} is a
pinned-auditor grade, and no agent grades its own family
(Section~\ref{sec:auditing}). They are, however, the only second reading of
the same transcripts on record, which is why we use them to measure how much

\subsection{Re-grading an easy-tier sweep}

% src: notes/Analysis/2026-07-23 Pinned Sonnet-5 Replay (sweep 2687371).md:9-11,107-118
An easy-tier sweep of Qwen3.6-27B was graded by that model during the sweep and
re-graded in full by the pinned auditor on 2026-07-23. Both graders scored 32
of the 34 runs, and the paired comparison is over those 32. The mean score is
4.66 under the sweep's own grader and 3.97 under the pinned auditor. The
sweep's grader scored higher on 10 runs, equal on 20, and lower on 2, for a
mean difference of 0.69 (Wilcoxon $p = 0.0108$). Over all 34 pinned audits the
mean is 3.91.

% src: notes/Analysis/2026-07-23 Pinned Sonnet-5 Replay (sweep 2687371).md:109-114,132-142,147-158
The reproduced set stays the same size but its membership shifts by two: one
run loses the verdict and one gains it. The disqualified count rises from 2 to
6, as does the number of runs with a high-severity flag. All four added
disqualifications are flagged \texttt{other\_provenance\_break} or
\texttt{wrong\_split\_scale\_dataset}, and each cites a specific break. One
report, for example, claims a 2{,}427-sample evaluation when the only value
established by execution evidence comes from a 5-sample probe.

% src: notes/Analysis/2026-07-23 Pinned Sonnet-5 Replay (sweep 2687371).md:120-131, prompts/prompt_audit.txt:88-101
Grader identity is not the only difference between the arms. On 2026-07-22 we
amended the audit prompt's severity rules so that a released results file the
agent consumes and discloses caps the score band without raising a flag. Of
the 33 grades in the first arm, 32 predate that amendment, while all 34 pinned
audits postdate it, so grader identity and prompt version moved together in
this comparison. The amended rule applied to exactly one run of the 34, at low
severity, which does not reach the score-0 cap, so the remainder of the
difference is attributable to the grader.

\subsection{Re-grading a medium-tier sweep}

% src: notes/Analysis/2026-08-03 Medium Sweep 2690187 (MiniMax, pinned Sonnet).md:9-10,25-27,266-277, notes/Analysis/medium-sweep-2690187-minimax-runs.json
A medium-tier sweep of MiniMax-M2.7 was graded the same way and re-graded by
the pinned auditor on 2026-08-03, again covering all 32 runs. Here the pinned
auditor's reproduced set is a strict subset of the sweep grader's, with no run
promoted into it. Both graders disqualify exactly 10 runs, but they agree on
only 4 of them: six runs are disqualified by the sweep's grader alone and six
by the pinned auditor alone.

% src: notes/Analysis/2026-08-03 Medium Sweep 2690187 (MiniMax, pinned Sonnet).md:277-284
The verdict changes on 16 of the 32 runs and the score on 20. Score
correlation across all 32 runs is $r = 0.657$. Restricted to the 16 runs where
neither grader applied the score-0 cap, it rises to $r = 0.863$, with subset
means of 6.12 and 5.69.

\subsection{Where the graders disagree}

% src: notes/Analysis/2026-08-03 Medium Sweep 2690187 (MiniMax, pinned Sonnet).md:286-291, notes/Analysis/2026-07-23 Pinned Sonnet-5 Replay (sweep 2687371).md:147-158, notes/Analysis/2026-08-13 Hard Sweep 2918306 (DSv4, pinned Sonnet).md:456
Both measurements locate the disagreement in the same part of the scale. Away
from the cap the continuous score is reliable ($r = 0.863$), and the top of
the scale is stable, since the earlier reproductions survive the grader swap
as a strict subset. The unstable part is the score-0 cap. No second pass of
the pinned auditor over a single sweep is on record, so the within-grader
stability of the cap remains unmeasured.

Two reporting rules follow, and we apply both. First, whenever we report a
disqualification count we report it as a rubric behavior and note this
instability alongside it, given that only 4 of the 10 disqualifications in
the medium-tier sweep are shared by both graders. Second, we compare mean
audit scores only within a single grader, since the grader swap alone shifts
this sweep's mean by 0.5 points on identical transcripts.

\subsection{Human evaluation}

% src: iclr2027_conference.tex:413-415, notes/Writing/Section Pointers.md:20, notes/TODO List.md:86-87
The expert-grader study is still pending, and its result is the agreement
figure quoted in Section~\ref{sec:auditing}. In the planned design, expert
graders score a held-out sample of runs together with cases that contain
injected provenance breaks, and we report agreement with the human labels as
a kappa and an F1, plus recall on the injected cases. Until that study runs,
the code-enforced cap is evidence only that a flagged run cannot pass. It says
nothing about flags the auditor missed or scores it set too harshly.
