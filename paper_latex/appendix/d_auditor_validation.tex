\section{Auditor Validation}
\label{app:auditor-validation}

% src: notes/Analysis/2026-07-23 Pinned Sonnet-5 Replay (sweep 2687371).md, notes/Analysis/2026-08-03 Medium Sweep 2690187 (MiniMax, pinned Sonnet).md
This appendix records what is known about grader noise in the audit scores of
Section~\ref{sec:auditing}, measured by re-grading sweeps that had already
been graded once.

\subsection{What pinning the auditor fixes}

% src: src/reprocli_claude/__main__.py:1-19,74-81,265-276, src/reprocli_vllm/config/config.py:42-43, rubric_audit.md:1-15, src/reprocli_vllm/audit/audit.py:35-63
Four inputs are held constant across every grade of record, the grader model
id written onto every verdict row, the rubric file with its freeze date of 16
July 2026, the audit prompt rendered per run from that rubric text and the
pinned claim, and the shared finalizer that applies the integrity adjustment
of Appendix~\ref{app:rubric}, with the grading client and its decoding
settings given in Appendix~\ref{app:models}.

% src: src/reprocli_claude/__main__.py:193-201,282-295, src/reprocli_vllm/runtime/audit_sink.py:85-104,124-129, notes/Analysis/2026-07-23 Pinned Sonnet-5 Replay (sweep 2687371).md:18-28
A grading pass also carries an identity of its own, the grader model name
joined to a UTC timestamp, and every row and transcript event the pass writes
is keyed by it. That tag makes a second pass observable. Without it a re-grade
resolves to the first pass's row and inherits the older verdict.

\subsection{Protocols}

Two validations re-grade runs without re-executing them, so the evidence
bundles, the pinned claims, the frozen rubric text, and the score scale are
identical in both arms and only the grader changes. Both sweeps ran after the
13 July 2026 dataset freeze, and the pinned arm of each is graded by Claude
Sonnet 5.

\subsection{Changing the grader on an easy-tier sweep}

% src: notes/Analysis/2026-07-23 Pinned Sonnet-5 Replay (sweep 2687371).md:9-11,107-118
An easy-tier sweep of Qwen3.6-27B was graded by that model during the sweep
and re-graded by the pinned auditor on 23 July 2026, 34 runs of 34. Both
graders scored 32 of those runs. The mean score is 4.66 under the sweep's own
grader and 3.97 under the pinned auditor. The sweep's grader scored higher on
10 runs, equal on 20, and lower on 2, and the mean difference of 0.69 carries
a Wilcoxon $p = 0.0108$. Across all 34 pinned audits the mean is 3.91.

% src: notes/Analysis/2026-07-23 Pinned Sonnet-5 Replay (sweep 2687371).md:109-114,132-142,147-158
The reproduced set holds its size, and its membership moves by two, with one
run losing the verdict and one gaining it. The count of runs carrying a
high-severity flag moves with the disqualified count, from 2 to 6. All four
added disqualifications are flagged \texttt{other\_provenance\_break} or
\texttt{wrong\_split\_scale\_dataset}, and each cites a specific break, such as
a report claiming a 2{,}427-sample evaluation whose only backed value comes
from a 5-sample probe.

% src: notes/Analysis/2026-07-23 Pinned Sonnet-5 Replay (sweep 2687371).md:120-131, prompts/prompt_audit.txt:88-101
Grader identity is not the only difference between the arms. The audit
prompt's severity rules were amended on 22 July 2026, so that a released
results file which the agent consumes and discloses caps the score band
without raising a flag. 32 of the 33 grades in the first arm predate that
amendment and all 34 pinned audits postdate it, so grader identity and prompt
version moved together. The amended rule fired on exactly one run of 34, at
low severity, which does not reach the score-0 cap, so the rest of the
difference is attributable to the grader.

\subsection{Changing the grader on a medium-tier sweep}

% src: notes/Analysis/2026-08-03 Medium Sweep 2690187 (MiniMax, pinned Sonnet).md:9-10,25-27,266-277, notes/Analysis/medium-sweep-2690187-minimax-runs.json
A medium-tier sweep of MiniMax-M2.7 was graded the same way and re-graded by
the pinned auditor on 3 August 2026, 32 runs of 32. The pinned auditor's
reproduced set is a subset of the sweep grader's, with no run promoted into it. Both graders
disqualify exactly 10 runs and they agree on 4 of those. Six papers are
disqualified only by the sweep's grader and six only by the pinned auditor.

% src: notes/Analysis/2026-08-03 Medium Sweep 2690187 (MiniMax, pinned Sonnet).md:277-284
The verdict changes on 16 of the 32 runs and the score changes on 20. Score
correlation across all 32 is $r = 0.657$. Across the 16 runs where neither
grader applied the score-0 cap it is $r = 0.863$, with subset means of 6.12
and 5.69.

\subsection{Where the disagreement sits}

% src: notes/Analysis/2026-08-03 Medium Sweep 2690187 (MiniMax, pinned Sonnet).md:286-291, notes/Analysis/2026-07-23 Pinned Sonnet-5 Replay (sweep 2687371).md:147-158, notes/Analysis/2026-08-13 Hard Sweep 2918306 (DSv4, pinned Sonnet).md:456
The two measurements put the disagreement in one place. The continuous score
is reliable where the cap does not fire, at $r = 0.863$, and the top of the
scale is stable, with the earlier reproductions surviving the swap as a strict
subset. The score-0 cap is the unstable part. No second pass of the pinned
auditor over one sweep is on record, so the within-grader stability of the cap
is unmeasured.

% src: notes/Analysis/2026-08-03 Medium Sweep 2690187 (MiniMax, pinned Sonnet).md:293-300,332-335
Two reporting rules follow, and we apply both. We report a disqualification
count as a rubric behaviour with this instability attached, since 4 of 10
disqualifications are shared between the two graders of the medium-tier sweep.
We compare a mean audit score only within one grader, since the grader swap
alone moves this sweep's mean by 0.5 points over identical transcripts.

\subsection{Human validation}

% src: iclr2027_conference.tex:413-415, notes/Writing/Section Pointers.md:20, notes/TODO List.md:86-87
The expert-grader study is pending and its result is the agreement figure
quoted in Section~\ref{sec:auditing}. The design on file has expert graders
score a held-out sample of runs alongside cases carrying injected provenance
breaks, and reports agreement as a kappa and an F1 against the human labels,
with recall on the injected cases. Until that study runs, the code-enforced
cap is evidence that a flagged run cannot pass and is no evidence about a flag
the auditor missed or a score it set too harshly.
