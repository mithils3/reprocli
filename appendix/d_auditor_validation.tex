\section{Auditor Validation}
\label{app:auditor-validation}

% src: notes/Analysis/2026-07-23 Pinned Sonnet-5 Replay (sweep 2687371).md, notes/Analysis/2026-08-03 Medium Sweep 2690187 (MiniMax, pinned Sonnet).md
This appendix reports what we know about grader noise in the audit scores of
Section~\ref{sec:auditing}, measured by re-grading sweeps that had already
been graded once. We write grader for any model that scored a run and auditor
for the pinned grader whose scores the paper reports.

\subsection{Why the auditor is pinned}

% src: src/reprocli_claude/__main__.py:1-19,74-81,265-276, src/reprocli_vllm/config/config.py:42-43, rubric_audit.md:1-15, src/reprocli_vllm/audit/audit.py:35-63
We hold four inputs constant across every grade of record. They are the grader
model ID written onto every verdict row, the rubric file with its freeze date
of 2026-07-16, the audit prompt rendered per run from that rubric text and the
pinned claim, and the shared verdict code that applies the integrity
adjustment of Appendix~\ref{app:rubric}. The grading client and its decoding
settings are given in Appendix~\ref{app:models}.

% src: src/reprocli_claude/__main__.py:193-201,282-295, src/reprocli_vllm/runtime/audit_sink.py:85-104,124-129, notes/Analysis/2026-07-23 Pinned Sonnet-5 Replay (sweep 2687371).md:18-28
Each grading pass also carries an identity of its own, the grader model name
joined to a UTC timestamp. Every row and transcript event the pass writes is
keyed by that identity. The identity makes a second pass observable. Without
it a re-grade resolves to the first pass's row and inherits the older verdict.

\subsection{Validation protocol}

% src: notes/Analysis/2026-07-23 Pinned Sonnet-5 Replay (sweep 2687371).md:9-11, notes/Analysis/2026-08-03 Medium Sweep 2690187 (MiniMax, pinned Sonnet).md:9-10,18-27, notes/Analysis/2026-07-20 Easy Sweep 2652648 (MiniMax repair).md:18-20, notes/Analysis/easy-sweep-2687371-qwen3-runs.json, notes/Analysis/medium-sweep-2690187-minimax-runs.json, rubric_audit.md:1-5
In both validations we re-grade runs without re-executing them, so the run
bundles, the pinned claims, the frozen rubric text, and the score scale are
identical in both arms and only the grader changes. Both sweeps ran after the
2026-07-13 dataset freeze, and the pinned arm of each is graded by Claude
Sonnet 5.

% src: iclr2027_conference.tex:385-390, notes/Analysis/2026-08-03 Medium Sweep 2690187 (MiniMax, pinned Sonnet).md:12-16,25-27
The other arm of each comparison is a grade the agent model produced over its
own family's runs. Those grades are not results of this paper. Every score
reported in Section~\ref{subsec:results} is a pinned-auditor grade, and no

are the only second reading of the same transcripts on record, so we use them
to measure how much two graders disagree. Table~\ref{tab:auditor-validation}

\subsection{Re-grading an easy-tier sweep}

% src: notes/Analysis/2026-07-23 Pinned Sonnet-5 Replay (sweep 2687371).md:9-11,107-118
An easy-tier sweep of Qwen3.6-27B was graded by that model during the sweep
and re-graded by the pinned auditor on 2026-07-23, 34 runs of 34. Both
graders scored 32 of those runs. The mean score is 4.66 under the sweep's own
grader and 3.97 under the pinned auditor. The sweep's grader scored higher on
10 runs, equal on 20, and lower on 2, with a mean difference of 0.69 at a Wilcoxon $p = 0.0108$. Across all 34 pinned audits the mean is
3.91.

% src: notes/Analysis/2026-07-23 Pinned Sonnet-5 Replay (sweep 2687371).md:109-114,132-142,147-158
The reproduced set holds its size, and its membership moves by two, with one
run losing the verdict and one gaining it. The count of runs carrying a high-severity flag moves with the disqualified
count, from 2 to 6. All four
added disqualifications are flagged \texttt{other\_provenance\_break} or
\texttt{wrong\_split\_scale\_dataset}, and each cites a specific break, such as
a report claiming a 2{,}427-sample evaluation whose only value established by
execution evidence comes from a 5-sample probe.

% src: notes/Analysis/2026-07-23 Pinned Sonnet-5 Replay (sweep 2687371).md:120-131, prompts/prompt_audit.txt:88-101
Grader identity is not the only difference between the arms. We amended the
audit prompt's severity rules on 2026-07-22, so that a released results file
which the agent consumes and discloses caps the score band without raising a
flag. In the first arm, 32 of the 33 grades predate that amendment and all 34
pinned audits postdate it, so grader identity and prompt version moved together. The amended rule matched on exactly one run of 34, at low severity,
which does not reach the score-0 cap, so the rest of the difference is
attributable to the grader.

\subsection{Re-grading a medium-tier sweep}

% src: notes/Analysis/2026-08-03 Medium Sweep 2690187 (MiniMax, pinned Sonnet).md:9-10,25-27,266-277, notes/Analysis/medium-sweep-2690187-minimax-runs.json
A medium-tier sweep of MiniMax-M2.7 was graded the same way and re-graded by
the pinned auditor on 2026-08-03, 32 runs of 32. The pinned auditor's
reproduced set is a subset of the sweep grader's, with no run promoted into
it. Both graders disqualify exactly 10 runs and they agree on 4 of those. Six
runs are disqualified only by the sweep's grader and six only by the pinned
auditor.

% src: notes/Analysis/2026-08-03 Medium Sweep 2690187 (MiniMax, pinned Sonnet).md:277-284
The verdict changes on 16 of the 32 runs and the score changes on 20. Score
correlation across all 32 is $r = 0.657$. Across the 16 runs where neither
grader applied the score-0 cap the correlation is $r = 0.863$, with subset
means of 6.12 and 5.69.

\subsection{Where the graders disagree}

% src: notes/Analysis/2026-08-03 Medium Sweep 2690187 (MiniMax, pinned Sonnet).md:286-291, notes/Analysis/2026-07-23 Pinned Sonnet-5 Replay (sweep 2687371).md:147-158, notes/Analysis/2026-08-13 Hard Sweep 2918306 (DSv4, pinned Sonnet).md:456
The two measurements locate the disagreement in the same part of the scale.
The continuous score is reliable where the cap is not applied, at
$r = 0.863$, and the top of the scale is stable, with the earlier
reproductions surviving the swap as a strict subset. The score-0 cap is the
unstable part. No second pass of the pinned auditor over one sweep is on record, so the
within-grader stability of the cap is unmeasured.

% src: notes/Analysis/2026-08-03 Medium Sweep 2690187 (MiniMax, pinned Sonnet).md:293-300,332-335
Two reporting rules follow, and we apply both. We report a disqualification
count as a rubric behavior and report this instability with it, since 4 of 10
disqualifications are shared between the two graders of the medium-tier
sweep. We compare a mean audit score only within one grader, since the grader
swap alone moves this sweep's mean by 0.5 points over identical transcripts.

\subsection{Human evaluation}

% src: iclr2027_conference.tex:413-415, notes/Writing/Section Pointers.md:20, notes/TODO List.md:86-87
The expert-grader study is pending and its result is the agreement figure
quoted in Section~\ref{sec:auditing}. In the planned design, expert graders
score a held-out sample of runs alongside cases with injected provenance
breaks, and we report agreement as a kappa and an F1 against the human
labels, with recall on the injected cases. Until that study runs, the
code-enforced cap is evidence that a flagged run cannot pass. It is no
evidence about a flag the auditor missed or a score the auditor set too
harshly.
